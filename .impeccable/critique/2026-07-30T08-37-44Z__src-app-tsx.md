## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: authored, not category-interchangeable.** This does not read as a template. The evidence: a 19vw serif name with per-letter opsz/wght response to cursor proximity; an asymmetric hero where the text block is offset to 38% rather than centered or split 50/50; a dark constant-token slab band mid-page that breaks the paper rhythm; paper grain via multiply blend in light and screen in dark; a credibility marquee carrying real numbers (Google Search, PCI DSS, 3.2M users, $1M/mo) instead of client logos. The typographic system (Fraunces display + Inter body + JetBrains Mono used sparingly for years and labels) is coherent and the clay/olive pairing is unusual in a developer portfolio, which mostly converges on blue-on-white or neon-on-black. Copy carries a point of view — section titles are sentences, not nouns.

Where it is less specific: the Experience section repeats one structural pattern (240px meta column + 1fr detail) six times with no variation in rhythm, and Skills is a five-row definition list of the same shape. Both are correct and readable, but they are the two places where the page stops making decisions and starts filling a grid.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` over `src/` returned exit 0 with 2 warnings, both `overused-font` (category: slop) at `src/index.css:1` and `src/index.css:62`, flagging Fraunces and Inter as faces that "no longer feel distinctive."

I read both as **false positives in this context**. The rule targets defaults picked without thought; here Fraunces is loaded as a variable font and its opsz/wght axes are driven live by cursor proximity in `VariableName.tsx`, which is the opposite of a default choice — the typeface is the interaction. Inter is confined to body copy where neutrality is the job. The detector cannot see that distinction. Worth noting rather than acting on.

**Visual overlays:** not attempted. Browser script injection and the bundled live-server overlay path were skipped in favor of direct Playwright screenshots and computed-style measurement, so no user-visible overlay exists. Fallback signal: four full-page screenshots (desktop light/dark, mobile light, desktop viewport) plus scripted computed-style sampling and a full axe-core run.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely good portfolio that is being undermined by one animation bug and one stale claim in its own documentation.

The craft is real: restraint, a committed typographic voice, warmth from material choices rather than decoration. The single biggest opportunity is not visual at all. It is that the entrance animation ignores `prefers-reduced-motion`, which simultaneously breaks a documented invariant, harms the exact users the project claims to care about, and produces the accessibility violation that contradicts the README's "0 violations" claim. Fixing one thing fixes all three.

## What's Working

**The hero's restraint.** Opening straight on the name at 19vw with no eyebrow, no stats grid, and no "Hi, I'm" is a confident decision most portfolios cannot make. The offset text block at `md:ml-[38%]` creates editorial asymmetry, and the left void is doing real compositional work rather than being unused space.

**Material honesty in the theming.** The grain overlay switching from `mix-blend-mode: multiply` at 0.4 opacity in light to `screen` at 0.06 in dark is the kind of detail that separates a themed site from a site with a dark mode. Combined with `slab` tokens deliberately held theme-independent for the contrast band, this is a design system that thought about both modes as first-class.

**Accessibility as architecture, not retrofit.** A translated skip link, `aria-pressed` plus a non-color underline state on both toggles, decorative project images correctly given `alt=""` inside `aria-hidden` wrappers to avoid duplicate link announcements, and a global `:focus-visible` ring with offset. The intent and most of the execution are well above the norm.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Hero ignores prefers-reduced-motion

**What:** `Hero.tsx` builds its entrance from a local `rise()` helper that sets `initial: { opacity: 0, filter: 'blur(6px)' }` → `animate: { opacity: 1, filter: 'blur(0px)' }` over 950ms, applied to seven elements with delays from 0.12s to 0.7s. Unlike `Reveal.tsx`, it never calls `useReducedMotion()`. The `<MotionConfig reducedMotion="user">` wrapper in `App.tsx` does not cover this: framer-motion's reduced-motion handling neutralizes transform and layout animations, not `opacity` or `filter`.

Measured directly, sampling computed styles every 300ms after mount:

```
prefers-reduced-motion: reduce        no-preference
t=0     opacity=0    blur(6px)        opacity=0    blur(6px)
t=300   opacity=0.46 blur(3.22px)     opacity=0.46 blur(3.25px)
t=600   opacity=0.88 blur(0.70px)     opacity=0.88 blur(0.74px)
t=900   opacity=0.99 blur(0.03px)     opacity=0.99 blur(0.03px)
t=1200  opacity=1    blur(0px)        opacity=1    blur(0px)
```

Identical. The preference has no effect on the hero.

**Why it matters:** A 950ms animated blur on 11rem display type is exactly the class of motion `prefers-reduced-motion` exists to suppress — large-area, high-contrast, slow. A vestibular-sensitive visitor who has set the preference at OS level still gets it. This also breaks a stated invariant: the repo documents reduced-motion paths in `Reveal`/`VariableName`/`ThemeToggle`, and `Hero` is the loudest motion on the page but is absent from that list.

**Fix:** Call `useReducedMotion()` in `Hero.tsx` and have `rise()` return `{}` (or `initial: false`) when it returns true, mirroring the early-return pattern already proven in `Reveal.tsx`. One hook, one conditional.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable animate`

### [P1] The accessibility claim is stale — axe is no longer clean

**What:** `CLAUDE.md` states "axe-core clean, WCAG 2.1 AA" and "EN/ES/FR × light/dark = 0 violations." A full run of the project's own `~/.pw-shot/axe-audit.mjs` now reports:

```
en/light: 1   en/dark: 0
es/light: 1   es/dark: 0
fr/light: 1   fr/dark: 0
Unique issues: [serious] color-contrast
```

The violation is on `.mt-14` (the hero's "Seven years, scrolled" cue): measured `#776b5c` on `#eae0d5` at 3.98:1, below the 4.5:1 requirement.

**Why it matters:** This is a real finding with a non-obvious cause. At rest that element computes to `rgb(107, 95, 80)` (`#6B5F50`), which is the tuned `muted` token at 4.7:1 and passes. The failure only exists *during* the entrance animation, because the cue carries `delay: 0.7s` on a 0.95s fade, so it sits at partial opacity for roughly 1.65s after load and axe sampled inside that window. So the tokens are fine and the animation is the defect — the same defect as P1. But the consequence is independent: anyone auditing this site with axe (a recruiter's tooling, a hiring team's a11y check, your own re-run) gets a serious violation, and the documentation says that cannot happen.

**Fix:** P1's reduced-motion guard removes the violation in the reduce case. To fix it unconditionally, either drop the opacity floor so text never renders below AA during transit (animate blur and transform only, holding opacity at 1), or shorten the cue's `delay`/`duration` so the sub-AA window is negligible. Then re-run the audit and update the claim in `CLAUDE.md` with the date.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] 31 em dashes per locale bundle, in a document aimed at recruiters

**What:** `grep -c "—"` returns exactly 31 in each of `src/i18n/en.ts`, `es.ts`, and `fr.ts`. Three of them land in the first viewport: the tagline ("secure, accessible web platforms — front to back") and twice in the summary.

**Why it matters:** You established two turns ago, in your own words, that em dashes read as AI-generated text and cost you credibility in hiring contexts — and we stripped them from the Turbo AI answers for that exact reason. This portfolio is the same kind of document, aimed at the same kind of reader, and it is denser in em dashes than the application was. The rule you wrote for yourself applies here more than anywhere.

**Fix:** Rewrite the copy in all three bundles using commas, semicolons, parentheses, or two sentences. This is content-layer only, so no component changes, and the TS build will not complain. Worth doing by hand rather than by regex, because a few of them want restructuring rather than substitution.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] No sense of place on a 12,961px page

**What:** At 390px the document is 12,961px tall, roughly 33 screens. The nav is fixed and its five links have a hover underline, but nothing indicates the current section, and there is no persistent way back to the top other than the logo.

**Why it matters:** Heuristic 1 is the page's weakest score for a concrete reason. A recruiter skimming on a phone has no map: no sense of how much is left, which of the six sections they are in, or how to return. The information is all excellent; the wayfinding is missing.

**Fix:** Scroll-spy the nav links against section anchors (IntersectionObserver, mark the active one with the existing underline treatment plus `aria-current="location"` so it is not color-only). Optionally add a thin scroll-progress rule under the fixed header — it would suit the editorial register better than a floating button.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P2] The Reveal stagger never staggers, and 950ms is slow for a scroll reveal

**What:** Every list passes `delay={i * 0.04}` (Experience, Skills) or `i * 0.05` (Projects) into `Reveal`, which fires on `whileInView` with `margin: '-90px'` and `once: true`, over a 0.95s duration.

**Why it matters:** Stagger only reads as stagger when siblings enter the viewport together. These do not: each Experience `<article>` is `py-12` with multiple bullet lines, so on any normal scroll they cross the trigger one at a time, seconds apart. The delay therefore produces no visual rhythm — it just adds up to 240ms of dead latency before content resolves, on top of a 950ms fade. The result is content that feels like it is catching up with the scroll. Separately, animating `filter: blur()` on roughly fifteen reveal wrappers is the most expensive way to buy that effect; blur forces repaints that transform and opacity do not.

**Fix:** Drop the per-index delay on scroll-triggered reveals and keep it only where siblings genuinely animate as a group (the hero). Bring the duration into the 350–500ms range. If the blur-to-sharp signature matters — and it is a nice signature — keep it but reduce the radius and confirm it stays smooth on a mid-range phone.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable animate`

## Minor Observations

- **Dead translated content.** `imageAlt` is defined for both projects in all three locale bundles but can never render: `Projects.tsx` only uses it in the `!p.live` branch, and both projects with images have `live` set, so they always take the `aria-hidden` path with `alt=""`. The `aria-hidden` decision is correct; the unused strings are maintenance debt across three files.
- **No `contentinfo` landmark.** The site `<footer>` lives inside `<section id="contact">`, so per HTML semantics it is a sectioning footer rather than the page's `contentinfo` landmark. axe does not flag this, but screen-reader users lose the ability to jump to the footer by landmark. Moving it out to be a direct child of `<body>` (or the top-level layout) restores it.
- **Project section imbalance.** Two of four projects carry images (El Mundo Conocido, Lyra); FinDash and TripSurprise do not. The repo bans identical card grids deliberately, so variation is intentional, but the current split loads the visual weight into the first half and leaves the section tapering off.
- **Font loading is render-blocking and third-party.** Three families come in via `@import` in `index.css`, which serializes behind CSS parse and hard-depends on Google Fonts at runtime. Self-hosting the two weights actually used would remove a third-party dependency and speed up first paint on the display type that is the whole hero.
- **Google Fonts is also a privacy consideration** for EU recruiters given how the FR locale is positioned; self-hosting resolves that too.
