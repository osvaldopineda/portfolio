## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: authored, not category-interchangeable.** The proof is concentrated: the Approach manifesto ("AI is Robin. I'm still Batman.", "I review code for the next human, not the compiler."), the untranslated "Hecho con ♥ en México" that survives every locale, the timeline's ticks-not-dots, the Skills colophon that runs as prose instead of a chip wall, and the documented refusal of a maroon accent because guinda reads institutional in Mexico. Swap the name out and the page breaks. Five structurally different section layouts in one page and not one card grid: this is why it does not read as generated.

Where it stops deciding and starts filling a grid:

- **Experience.** Six jobs, identical internal template, uniform typographic weight, 3,066px of constant cadence. Nothing tells a recruiter that Google and Fondeadora outweigh the rest. The most valuable content on the page is the least designed, with the right third of a 1440 viewport empty for all of it.
- **Skills.** Forty tools in four comma-runs of 9-10. The colophon form is a genuinely good idea, but no editorial judgment is applied inside it: "React.js" and "Prompt Engineering" sit at identical weight. It is a chip wall with the chips removed.
- **Contact's education triad.** `md:grid-cols-3`, three identical stacked blocks. The one identical-card grid on the page.
- **The hero role line.** `font-mono text-xs uppercase tracking-[0.2em] text-accent` is an uppercase tracked eyebrow in accent color, the exact pattern CLAUDE.md bans above sections, relocated 40px below the name. It also wraps as "AI-/DRIVEN" at 390px and "SAAS Y FINTECH / CON IA" at 768px.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json src public` exited 0 with **zero findings**. The in-page detector (injected successfully, 18 element groups) reported 15 `low-contrast` hits, 1 `all-caps-body`, 1 `tight-leading`, 1 `dark-glow`, 1 `repeating-stripes-gradient`.

## Overall Impression

This is a portfolio with a real point of view that has started to compete with itself. The type, the restraint and the writing are doing exactly what they should. The edition system, which is the newest and most technically impressive thing on the page, is the one feature spending the visitor's attention on the frame instead of the picture, and it brought two defects with it.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. It is that the Projects rail, the section carrying the proof of work, is unreachable in parts by keyboard, on a site whose central credibility claim is "resolved 50+ accessibility defects on Google Search". Fixing that removes the one contradiction between what the page says and what the page does.

## What's Working

**Hero information density.** Role, tagline, summary and both CTAs fit above the fold at 390×844 with no scroll and no compromise on the 74px name. Most portfolios spend the entire first screen on a name. This one answers what, at what level, and how to reach him in one viewport on a phone, which is exactly the arrival context.

**Section-shape variation actually held.** Timeline, manifesto cascade, colophon, horizontal rail, editorial index. Five structurally different layouts, none of them a card grid. The Skills colophon takes the most template-prone content type in the genre and refuses the template.

**Restraint that costs something.** No stats strip, no chips, no scroll cue, one accented heading, no em dashes. Each of those would have made the page look busier in a screenshot, and removing them is what makes the type carry. The blur-in reveal being replaced by a 10px rise is the same instinct applied to motion.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The Projects rail drops a project and puts keyboard focus off-screen

**What.** In `src/components/Projects.tsx`, `focusCard(i)` (line 122) maps card index linearly onto scroll: `top + (i / (projects.length - 1)) * travel`. But `x` is driven by `useTransform(scrollYProgress, [0,1], [0, -shift])` where `shift = track.scrollWidth - window.innerWidth` (line 104). The two mappings do not agree. Measured at 1440×900: focusing EXSIX lands the card at `x = -52` (clipped), El Mundo Conocido at `x = -704` (fully off-screen); from a different starting scroll the same link landed at `x = 2076`. The mapping is not even stable. Separately, **TripSurprise has no focusable descendant at all**, confirmed in source: it has no `live` and no `repo`, and `Shot` only renders an anchor when `p.live` exists (line 61), so Tab goes FinDash → El Mundo Conocido and skips a project entirely.

**Why it matters.** WCAG 2.4.7 and 2.4.11 failing in the section that carries the proof of work, on a site that sells accessibility expertise. A keyboard or screen-reader user reads a project's blurb and links while the card is not on screen, and never encounters TripSurprise at all.

**Fix.** Two changes in `Projects.tsx`. (1) Derive the focus target from measured geometry instead of index arithmetic: read the card's `offsetLeft` from the track and solve for the scroll position that puts it at the viewport's left padding, using the same `shift` the transform uses so the two cannot drift. (2) Give every card a focusable element: render the project name as a link when `p.live || p.repo`, and for status-only projects like TripSurprise give the `<article>` `tabIndex={0}` with an `aria-label`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The edition nudge interrupts the first read and dims the CTAs

**What.** `EditionNudge.tsx:55` fires driver.js 2,500ms after first paint, applies a 0.45 overlay over the entire hero including "Download CV" and "Email me", moves focus to the popover close button, and traps Tab in a three-stop cycle. Escape closes it but drops focus to `<body>`, resetting a keyboard user to the top of the document.

**Why it matters.** 2.5s is precisely when a recruiter is mid-summary, reading "banking-grade PCI DSS" and "accessibility on Google Search". The site stops them to talk about itself. For a keyboard or screen-reader user, focus is yanked into a modal they did not open, about a feature that is not why they came.

**Fix.** Replace the `setTimeout(…, 2500)` trigger with an `IntersectionObserver` that fires only once `#work` has entered the viewport, so the nudge arrives after the recruiter has read the hero and committed to scrolling. Add `onDestroyed: () => document.getElementById('edition-toggle')?.focus()` so Escape returns focus to the control the popover described. Keep the popover and its CTA: the mechanism is good, the timing is the defect.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] The three facts an international recruiter needs are the smallest type on the page

**What.** Location, timezone and work-authorization status appear only in the status line, 12px mono, at roughly y = 13,200 of a 13,666px page. `cv.profile.location` ("Mexico City, Mexico · GMT-6") is authored in all three locale bundles (`en.ts:10`) and **rendered by no component**, confirmed by grep across `src/components/` and `src/App.tsx`. It is dead content.

**Why it matters.** The audience arrives from a CV and LinkedIn, internationally, mostly on a phone. Their first three questions are what does he do, can he legally work for me, and can we overlap on calls. The site answers the first in 300ms and the other two only after 13,000px of scroll. "Open to remote global collaboration, B2B, and relocation / sponsorship" is the highest-value sentence on the page for a hiring manager in Berlin, and it is placed last.

**Fix.** Render `cv.profile.location` in `Hero.tsx`, in the block that already holds the role line: one quiet line under the summary, `text-sm text-muted`, before the CTA row. Copy already exists in all three bundles. No badge, no pill, no icon row.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The header collides with itself between 768 and roughly 830px

**What.** Measured live at 768×900: the wordmark's right edge and the first nav link's left edge are both at `x = 176` in EN and both at `x = 123` in ES, touching. "Contact"/"Contacto" ends at `x = 531` and the language toggle group starts at `x = 531`, also touching. In ES the wordmark additionally wraps to two lines. At 900px the same gaps measure 60px. `Nav.tsx` switches the full desktop nav on at `md:` (line 74, 91, 111, 119), before there is room for it.

**Why it matters.** 768 is iPad portrait and a common desktop window width. A recruiter opening the link on a tablet sees the name running into the navigation, on the element that frames every other section.

**Fix.** Move the desktop nav's breakpoint from `md:` to `lg:` in `Nav.tsx` (link row, `LanguageToggle` wrapper, hamburger, and the mobile menu panel), so 768-1023px keeps the mobile menu, which already handles this content well. Add `whitespace-nowrap` to the wordmark to stop the ES two-line wrap.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Dark mode erases the page's only structural break, and the slab is edition-blind

**What.** The Approach slab is `slab: '#1C1A17'`, theme-independent by design (`tailwind.config.js:31`). Dark `--paper` is `#171310` (`index.css:23`). Against each other the section edge is a barely visible seam and the band reads as continuous page; in light mode the same boundary is the most dramatic moment on the site. Separately, `HowIWork.tsx:12` hardcodes `text-[#A3B3F5]`, so the page's one accented heading stays Editorial ultramarine inside Riso and Terminal, confirmed visually in Riso where the slab shows warm black and periwinkle with no green and no fluor pink.

**Why it matters.** A first-time visitor with no stored preference gets `prefers-color-scheme`, and a large share of that audience is in dark mode, so a large share never experiences the page's rhythm change and Approach, the most Osvaldo-specific writing on the site, arrives with no framing. The hardcoded accent separately means the biggest section on the page ignores the edition system, which undercuts the "three complete design systems" claim more than any other detail.

**Fix.** Keep `slab` constant in light but give `.dark` a distinct value (a warmer, lighter ink around `#26221D`), or invert the relationship so the band is the raised surface in dark. Replace the hardcoded `#A3B3F5` with a `--slab-accent` variable defined per edition in `index.css` (ultramarine, fluor pink, amber phosphor), so Riso's second ink finally appears where it is most needed.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable colorize`

### [P2] Project card links are 20px tall on mobile

**What.** Measured at 390 width: "Live demo" (×4) renders at 86.9 × **20px** and "Code" (×3) at 56.2 × **20px**. Nav chrome is also small: the CV link is 51.3 × 32, the edition and theme buttons 36 × 36, the menu button 40 × 40. Inline prose links in Experience (15px tall) are exempt under the inline exception and are not counted here.

**Why it matters.** 20px fails WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (24px minimum, AA) unless the spacing exception applies, which was not measured. These are the links to the actual work, tapped one-handed on a phone by the primary audience.

**Fix.** Add vertical padding to the `Links` component's anchors in `Projects.tsx` (`py-2 -my-2` keeps the visual rhythm while growing the hit area) and give the nav CV link the same treatment. Do not change the type size.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

**Casey (distracted, one-handed, slow connection).** The hero is excellent for Casey: everything above the fold at 390×844. ## Minor Observations

- **Copying the hero name yields garbage.** `getSelection().toString()` over the `<h1>` returns `"OsvaldoOsvaldo\nPinedaPineda."`: the `sr-only` label and the per-letter `aria-hidden` spans both land in the selection, and Cmd+F for "Osvaldo Pineda" does not match the visible name. Add `select-none` to the decorative letter spans in `VariableName.tsx`.
- **framer-motion warning on every load** (the only console warning; zero errors): `Please ensure that the container has a non-static position`. Source is `Projects.tsx:115`, `useScroll({ target: sectionRef })` on a `<div>` with only an inline height and no `position: relative`. framer-motion states scroll offsets may compute incorrectly, which is plausibly related to the rail's focus-mapping drift.
- **Images have no `width`/`height`, no `srcset`, no `decoding`.** Six lazy JPEGs, largest 178.5KB, rendered at 502×314 CSS px from 960×600 sources, so effectively 1x on a 2× display. Missing intrinsic dimensions means layout shift as each card loads.
- **Alt text is split 2/6.** `la-casa` and `tripsurprise` carry descriptive alt; `exsix`, `lyra`, `findash` and `mundo-conocido` are `alt=""`. Both patterns are individually defensible; the split inside one component is not.
- Riso remaps `.serif` to expanded Archivo but leaves `.serif-soft` as Bricolage, so the tagline, the Contact email and the Approach accent render in a face that appears nowhere else in that edition. Terminal remaps both. Strip Riso's palette and misregistration and its layout is Editorial unchanged: it is the least finished of the three.
- **Stale comments.** `index.css:214` still says "Fraunces is Edición I only. Riso sets display in heavy Geist"; both fonts are gone. `Skills.tsx`'s header comment still says "the group name in italic display type"; Bricolage has no italics.
- On mobile the vertical project list orders text → stack → links → screenshot, so the CTA appears before the visitor has seen what the thing looks like. The rail's order (screenshot first) is the better one.
- Over the dark slab the fixed header stays `bg-paper/80`, producing a light strip across the ink band. Defensible as a paper edge, but a heading scrolling under a translucent light bar on a dark field reads as an accident.
- `TerminalBackdrop.tsx:25` reads `matchMedia` once at mount, so toggling the OS reduced-motion setting mid-session will not stop the rAF loop until reload. Verified otherwise clean: under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, `getAnimations()` returns 0 across all three editions and the canvas is byte-identical across a 900ms gap.

## Questions to Consider

1. The edition system costs a permanent header slot, a first-visit modal, three token worlds and a rotation rule. Six real project screenshots already prove he can build design systems. What does Edition II tell a hiring manager that the EXSIX and La Casa screenshots do not, and if the answer is "nothing, it is for other engineers", is the recruiter really the audience of this page?
2. If a recruiter forwards this link and says "look at the print-shop one", the hiring manager sees Terminal. Rotation between visits optimizes for the returning visitor. How many visitors return, versus arrive once from a CV link and forward it?
3. Approach is the only section that could not have been written by anyone else. It sits behind 3,000px of reverse-chronological resume and, in dark mode, arrives with no visual announcement at all. What is the argument for Experience going first, other than that resumes do?
4. Skills lists forty tools with no ranking. If a recruiter reads exactly one line of that section, which line should it be, and does the current design let him choose which one?
5. Two constraints worth pushing back on. **"No scroll cue"** was justified as "you know what scroll is": true for vertical, false for the rail, where a vertical gesture produces horizontal motion for 5,609px. A card index is not a bouncing chevron. **"No uppercase tracked eyebrow"** is banned above sections but implemented in the hero, where it also wraps badly at 390 and 768. Enforce the rule in the hero too, or drop it from the list, but the current state is the tell you were removing, moved up 40 pixels.
